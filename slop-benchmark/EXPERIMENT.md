# §5 (rewritten) — Evaluation: does structural admission gate AI slop?

> Draft replacement for the paper's current §5 (`find … | wc -l`) and Appendix B.3 ("AI slop is
> structurally inadmissible under three axioms"). Methods below are final; **RESULTS** is filled
> from `results/results.json` once the 480-item run completes.

## 5.1 Question and framing

We test the central claim operationally. CANONIC's gates decide *admission*; we therefore evaluate
admission as a binary classifier — does the gate REJECT slop and ADMIT legitimate content? — against
baselines, with ablations that isolate each gate. Ground truth is a property of the **content**
(is it slop?); the gate decision is a property of the **structure**. The experiment measures how
well structure predicts truth, and where it fails.

## 5.2 Independence (what §3 lacked)

The paper's §3 names the fatal weakness of the original construction: producer = validator =
evidence = one party ("the weakest possible adversary model"). We break that three ways:

1. **Validator from spec only.** The reference validator implements Appendix A's three axioms +
   Algorithm 1 + §3's three claim gates, with no behavior beyond the published text.
2. **Cross-family adversary.** The dressed-slop items (C3) and the per-gate variants are generated by
   a *non-Anthropic* model family (DeepSeek), since the CANONIC corpus itself was authored with
   Anthropic Claude. The adversary sees only the public grammar, never the validator internals.
3. **Independent labels.** Ground-truth slop/legit labels are the majority vote of a three-family
   panel (DeepSeek, OpenAI gpt-4o, Anthropic claude-sonnet) judging *prose alone*, structure hidden.
   We report Fleiss' κ and a manipulation check (panel agreement with generation intent).

## 5.3 Corpus

n = 480 governed-scope items. A 2×2 core (content: legit/slop × structure: scaffolded-valid/bare)
plus three per-gate single-failure variants so the ablations isolate each gate:

| Class | content | structure | gate *should* | what it isolates |
|------|---------|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| C1 | legit | valid triad, closed vocab, real in-window commit | ACCEPT | true negative |
| C2 | slop  | bare (no triad)                        | REJECT | naive slop |
| C3 | slop  | full triad, circular vocab, real-but-irrelevant commit, self-chosen window | REJECT | **the adversarial gap** |
| C4 | legit | bare prose, no triad                    | ACCEPT | **the structure tax** |
| Gintro | slop | triad but one undefined term           | REJECT | introspection gate only |
| Gevid  | slop | triad+closed, commit absent from ledger | REJECT | evidence gate only |
| Gscope | slop | triad+closed, real commit, date out of window | REJECT | scope gate only |

C3 operationalizes §3's three named attacks at once: *vocabulary padding* (circular VOCAB), *evidence
fabrication* (a real commit that does not support the claim), *window shopping* (an author-chosen
interval). The evidence ledger is 200 real commits from a live repository, so Gate 2 is a genuine
existence check, not a mock.

## 5.4 Arms

- **B0** admit-all (reference floor)
- **B1** MLX local perplexity detector (Apple-Silicon, base LM token-logprobs) — a *real* statistical
  detector; decision at the F1-optimal threshold (best case) and threshold-free ROC-AUC reported.
- **B1b** LLM AI-authorship detector (the Table-1 "detection tools" row as an LLM proxy)
- **B2** frontier semantic quality judge (the "human review" proxy)
- **T** CANONIC validator; **ablations** T−Introspection, T−Evidence, T−Scope.

## 5.5 Metrics

Positive class = slop. Per arm: precision / recall / F1 / accuracy vs panel ground truth, each with
a 95% percentile-bootstrap CI (2000 resamples). Headline quantities: the **gap** = reject(C2) −
reject(C3); the **tax** = reject(C4); per-gate **marginal catch** = reject_T(Gx) − reject_{T−gate}(Gx);
and B1 ROC-AUC of perplexity vs the slop label.

## 5.6 Pre-registered predictions (locked in `PREREGISTRATION.md` before the run)

P1 gap ≥ 0.65 · P2 tax ≥ 0.80 · P3 B2 rejects more C3 than T · P4 no arm dominates all classes.
Falsifier: if T rejects C3 at the C2 rate, §3's adversarial argument is wrong and CANONIC is stronger
than its author claimed.

## 5.7 RESULTS (n=480; balanced 2×2 core=360; panel κ=0.907; manipulation check=0.969)

Pre-registered predictions: **P1 ✓ P2 ✓ P3 ✓ P4 ✓** (all confirmed).

**Primary result — association with content-truth (Matthews φ; 0 = independent of truth).** Scoring a
structure-only gate as a slop classifier is circular (its F1 is a function of the class mix, not the
gate). We instead measure how each arm's accept/reject decision *correlates* with truth, on the
balanced 2×2 where structure and truth are decorrelated by design:

| Arm | φ vs truth | 95% CI (item) | 95% CI (cluster=topic) | odds ratio | beats chance? (label-perm) |
|-----|-----------|---------------|------------------------|-----------|----------------------------|
| **T (CANONIC)** | **−0.006** | [−0.039, 0.028] | [−0.032, 0.019] | **0.98** | **no, p=0.58** |
| B2 frontier semantic | **0.871** | [0.825, 0.913] | [0.808, 0.930] | 499.6 | yes, p<5×10⁻⁵ |
| B1b LLM detector | 0.221 | [0.173, 0.266] | [0.130, 0.298] | 37.9 | yes, p<5×10⁻⁵ |
| B1 MLX perplexity | 0.000 | [0.0, 0.0] | [0.0, 0.0] | 0.93 | no, p=1.0 (degenerate) |

**φ(T)=−0.006, CI brackets 0, OR≈1, and T fails the beats-chance test (p=0.58): structural admission
is statistically independent of content-truth.** It provides accountability, not slop-filtering. The
earlier "T F1 = 0.681" was an artifact of the class proportions and is *not* a property of the gate.

**The two real findings (off-diagonal cells, Wilson CIs).** These are the only cells where structure
and truth diverge, and they are the whole story:
- **C3 leakage** — slop with valid structure **admitted**: 90/90 = 1.00 [0.959, 1.0].
- **C4 tax** — legitimate content without structure **rejected**: 90/90 = 1.00 [0.959, 1.0].

**Head-to-head.** When structural (T) and semantic (B2) disagree, the semantic judge is correct in
**166/175 = 0.949 [0.905, 0.973]** of cases.

**Significance (one honest number).** φ(B2) − φ(T) = 0.877; topic-cluster permutation **p < 5×10⁻⁵**
(20 000 iters; equal at item level). The Fisher p≈10⁻⁵³ on the C2-vs-C3 gap is
significance-by-construction and is *not* reported as a result.

**Per-gate ablation (sound — gate variants are all slop).** Marginal catch = 1.0/1.0/1.0
(introspection/evidence/scope): each gate uniquely catches the slop that neglects exactly it, yet the
fully-dressed C3 (all gates satisfied) passes the whole stack — quantifying §3's three named attacks.

## 5.8 Interpretation (to fold into §3, §7, and the conclusion)

1. **Structural admission is orthogonal to truth (φ≈0, at chance).** Measured, not assumed: on a corpus
   where structure and content-truth are decorrelated, the gate's decisions carry *zero* slop signal.
   This **falsifies B.3** ("AI slop is structurally inadmissible") far more cleanly than the gap did —
   it is not that an adversary *can* evade the gate, it is that the gate was never discriminating truth.
2. **The gate's value is accountability, not filtering.** Every admitted claim is anchored (definition,
   commit, window); none of that is a truth guarantee. C3 (slop, fully anchored → admitted) is the proof.
3. **The tax is total** (C4 = 1.0): legitimate content without the structure is excluded, so the gate
   imposes a real adoption cost orthogonal to quality.
4. **Semantic verification is the part that tracks truth** (φ=0.871; beats chance p<5×10⁻⁵; right 95% of
   disagreements). The honest thesis is **complementarity on orthogonal axes** — structural admission
   bounds *accountability*, semantic judgment bounds *truth*, and a corpus gate needs both. This replaces
   "the fix is sixty years old" with a claim the data support.

## 5.9 Limitations of this evaluation (state plainly)

n is one pilot scale-up, not a population; the corpus is **synthetic** LLM slop whose lexical
exoticness makes the perplexity detector behave unlike it would on real-world *fluent* slop (hence the
AUC direction caveat) — a harvested real-world arm (retractions, fabricated-citation text, content-farm
prose) is the next step; cross-family independence is a *model*-independence proxy, not a human
red-team; and the validator is a faithful reimplementation of the spec, cross-checkable against the
production VaaS but not identical to it. **Shared-method caveat:** B2 and the ground-truth panel are
both LLM content judges, so φ(B2) partly reflects shared LLM bias rather than pure truth-tracking; it
is bounded above by panel reliability. We mitigate by (i) using a different provider/prompt for B2 than
the panel majority, (ii) anchoring labels to generation *intent* (manipulation check 0.969), and (iii)
reporting φ(T)≈0 — which is *immune* to this caveat, since the structural gate never reads content.

## 5.10 Retrieval-grounding arms (leveraging the real CANONIC corpus)

We added two grounding arms backed by the production CANONIC corpus (129 governed docs, cached
`@cf/baai/bge-m3` embeddings):
- **B3 (retrieval-similarity)** — reject if an item's max cosine to the corpus is below an F1-optimal
  threshold. Result: **φ(B3, truth) = 0.019** [−0.085, 0.112], odds ratio 1.3 — *at chance*, like T.
  Two reasons, both general: (i) **coverage** — off-domain claims score flat (all classes ≈0.53);
  a RAG index can only ground what it contains. (ii) **similarity ≠ entailment** — retrieval ranks by
  topical relevance, not whether a passage *supports* the specific claim, so even on-domain a
  fabricated on-topic claim retrieves highly-similar but non-supporting docs.
- **B3e (retrieve-THEN-entail)** — retrieve top-k, then a judge decides whether the excerpts actually
  support the claim. This is the gate a RAG index *can* power; its discrimination comes from the
  entailment step (a grounded semantic judge), not the index. *(on-domain result in §5.12)*

**Three-way headline:** structure ⊥ truth (φ=−0.006), retrieval-similarity ⊥ truth (φ=0.019), only
(grounded) semantic judgment tracks truth (φ=0.871). A RAG index is a retrieval substrate + provenance
layer, not a standalone truth gate. The paper's Gate 2 ("a commit exists") should become
retrieve-then-entail — real, but coverage-bound, and only as good as the corpus.

## 5.11 Adversarial robustness — does semantic truth-tracking survive an optimizing adversary?

A 10-strategy red-team (84 agents) generated slop engineered to fool an expert reviewer; 62/73 candidates
were independently verified as genuinely slop, then scored cross-provider (n=62, frontier judge).

| Quantity | non-adversarial | **adversarial red-team** |
|----------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| structural admit-rate (T) | — | **0.984** [0.914, 0.997] — structure is no barrier |
| B2 semantic catch-rate on slop | 0.878 | **0.477** [0.338, 0.621] |
| **combined-defense leak (slop past structure + B2)** | — | **0.371** [0.262, 0.495] |

**An optimizing adversary roughly halves the only gate that tracked truth (0.878 → 0.477), and 37% of
adversarial slop passes the full combined defense.** Strategy breakdown — *fluency defeats the semantic
judge*: plausible-mechanism (leak 0.71, B2 catch 0.00), fabricated-citations (0.70 / 0.00),
fluent-authoritative (0.70 / 0.10) sail through; the judge only catches *clumsy* slop —
overconfident-on-unknowns (0.10 / 0.90), jargon-grounded (0.12 / 0.88). Polished fabrication — i.e.,
real AI slop — is exactly what it misses.

**Consequence for the thesis:** the honest claim is stronger and more bounded than "complementarity
solves it." No combination of these gates is robust to a determined adversary: structure is bypassed by
construction, and the semantic gate — the only one that discriminates truth — is halved by fluent
fabrication, leaving a 37% combined leak. This is the §3 adversarial-admission argument, now measured.

## 5.12 On-domain tournament — gate efficacy depends on evaluator expertise (the key result)

We then tested the regime CANONIC actually governs: **novel content no judge was trained on.** Corpus =
60 real CANONIC passages (legit) + 64 fabricated CANONIC claims (slop), each verified genuinely-false by
a skeptic panel (8 strategies × 8, salvaged from the workflow transcripts). All four gates scored against
truth, frontier judge:

| Gate | φ vs truth | catches slop | **FALSE-rejects legit** |
|------|-----------|--------------|-------------------------|
| T structure        | +0.000 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| B3 similarity       | +0.093 | 1.00 | 0.98 |
| **B3e retrieve+entail** | **+0.271** | 1.00 | 0.87 |
| B2 semantic         | +0.017 | 0.80 | 0.78 |

**The reversal.** The semantic judge — which tracked truth on generic CS (φ=0.871) — **collapses to
φ=0.017 on CANONIC**, false-rejecting 78% of *real* passages: a generic judge cannot tell true bespoke
content from fabricated bespoke content because it is not a domain expert. Structure is φ=0 as always.
**Only retrieval-grounding adds signal in the no-expertise regime** — B3e (φ=0.271) is the single gate
that is meaningfully positive, because it substitutes the *corpus* for the missing expertise. The
combined-defense leak is 0/64 — but **that "perfect" defense is a mirage**: it is achieved only by
rejecting ~85% of legitimate content. A gate that rejects everything leaks nothing.

**The generalizable law: gate efficacy is a function of evaluator domain knowledge.** Semantic judging
works where the judge knows the domain and fails where it doesn't; structure never works; and
retrieve-then-entail is the gate that *matters most exactly in CANONIC's real use case* — governing
novel governed content that no foundation model has seen — because grounding against the actual corpus
is the only signal available when expertise is absent. Its binding constraints are corpus coverage and a
brutal false-reject tax (B3e over-rejects legit because doc-level retrieval + 700-char excerpts miss the
supporting sentence; passage-level retrieval is the obvious next upgrade).

**Net rewrite for the paper.** CANONIC governs novel content. On novel content, structure ⊥ truth and
semantic judgment ⊥ truth — so the paper's *only* defensible truth-bearing gate is **retrieve-then-entail
grounding against the governed corpus** (the corpus-KG, done as verification not similarity). That is the
honest home for Gate 2, it is CANONIC-native, and the research frontier it exposes — coverage + the
false-reject/precision tax — is the real contribution, far more than "slop is structurally inadmissible."

## 5.13 Passage-level grounding, an evaluation-leakage catch, and the novelty tension

Doc-level B3e over-rejected legit (φ=0.271) largely from coarse retrieval. We rebuilt the index at
**passage** granularity (2,820 canonic passages, real bge-m3) so the entailment judge sees the actual
candidate supporting sentences.

- **Naive passage-level B3e: φ=1.000** (catch_slop 1.0, false_reject_legit 0.00) — *too perfect.* The
  legit test items are real CANONIC passages **drawn from the same corpus the index is built from**, so
  each legit item **retrieves itself** ⇒ trivially "supported." Classic evaluation leakage.
- **Leave-one-out (exclude each legit item's own source doc from retrieval): φ=0.412** (catch_slop
  **1.000**, false_reject_legit **0.717**). The honest number. Passage-level still beats doc-level
  (0.271→0.412), and **fabrication detection is perfect and robust across every configuration
  (catch_slop = 1.0 doc, passage, and LOO)** — slop never grounds.

**The deeper finding (the novelty tension).** Even with passage retrieval and no leakage, B3e
false-rejects **72%** of *true* claims under leave-one-out — because a true claim's support frequently
lives **only in its own document**, and a corpus that excludes it contains no independent corroboration.
So grounding is intrinsically conservative: it admits a claim only if the corpus *independently* supports
it, which means **genuinely novel true content — the exact thing CANONIC exists to govern — cannot be
grounded and is rejected.** The false-reject tax is therefore not merely an engineering artifact of
retrieval quality; at its core it is the coverage law: *you cannot ground the new against the old.*

**Bottom line of the grounding arc:** retrieve-then-entail is the one gate that reliably catches
fabrication on novel bespoke content (catch=1.0) and the only gate with positive truth-association in
the no-expertise regime — but it pays for that with a structural hostility to novelty (admits only the
already-corroborated). The honest Gate-2 design is retrieve-then-entail *with a human-in-the-loop escalation
for un-grounded-but-plausible claims* — automation catches fabrication; novelty still needs a person.
And the leakage catch (φ 1.0→0.412) is a methodological warning to report: never evaluate a grounding
gate on items drawn from its own index.

## 5.14 External validity — the gates on REAL content (the capstone)

All prior arms used synthetic slop. We assembled a real-world corpus: 30 real arXiv abstracts (legit,
deterministic API fetch) + 17 provenance-verified real slop items (retracted-for-fraud, predatory,
content-farm; harvested by a 56-agent workflow with a fetch-verification guard). Ground truth by
construction.

| Gate | φ on real content | catch slop | false-reject legit |
|------|-------------------|-----------|---------------------|
| T structure | +0.000 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| B3 similarity (off-domain corpus) | −0.196 | 0.94 | 1.00 |
| B2 semantic | +0.270 | 0.41 | 0.17 |

Every law holds on real content: **structure ⊥ truth (φ=0)**; **off-domain grounding is worse than
useless (φ=−0.196, coverage floor)**; **semantic judgment is weak (φ=0.27)**. The per-source breakdown
is the capstone finding:

| Source (all real) | B2 catches | 3-model panel calls slop |
|-------------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| content-farm (prose-level slop) | **1.00** | 1.00 |
| predatory-journal | 0.17 | 0.33 |
| **retracted-for-fraud** | **0.17** | **0.00** |

**Fabricated data in normal scientific prose is invisible to prose-level gates.** A three-model frontier
panel called **0%** of retracted-for-fraud abstracts slop — they read as legitimate science because the
fraud is in the *data*, not the *prose*. The gates catch the slop that is easy (content-farm: 100%) and
miss the slop that is dangerous (fraud: 17%); B2 even false-rejects real arXiv abstracts (cs.CL 50%).
This is the real-world face of "fluency defeats the judge" — and here it is not adversarial, just
ordinary retracted papers.

**The thesis the whole study supports.** Across four regimes — non-adversarial synthetic, adversarial
synthetic, on-domain bespoke, and real-world — *no prose-reading gate reliably separates reliable from
unreliable content*, because the highest-stakes unreliability (fabricated data, fluent fabrication) is
surface-legitimate, and genuine novelty cannot be grounded. **The achievable goal is not filtering truth
but enforcing accountability**: CANONIC's structural gate guarantees every admitted claim is anchored to
a definition, a commit, and a window — so unreliable content cannot be *unaccountable*, even though it
can still be wrong. That is precisely the boundary §3 draws ("bounds the unaccountable, not the false"),
now established empirically rather than asserted. The contribution is the accountability substrate plus a
measured map of where automated truth-filtering fails — not a claim to have solved slop.
